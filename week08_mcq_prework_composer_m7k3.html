Question 1Confounding

In this DAG, variable C is a confounder of the relationship between exposure E and outcome Y. What does adjusting for C in a regression model do?

C affects both E and Y, and is not on the pathway from E to Y.
  • A. Blocks part of the true effect of E on Y
  • B. Closes a back-door path through C, helping isolate the E→Y association
  • C. Makes the model purely causal without any further assumptions
Answer: B
A confounder opens a spurious path E←C→Y. Adjusting for C holds C constant so the E coefficient is less distorted by that path.
Question 2Interpretation

A model is fitted as outcome ~ exposure + covariate. The coefficient on exposure is 2.4. What is the correct interpretation?

lm(outcome ~ exposure + covariate, data = dat) Estimate (Intercept) 10.0 exposure 2.4 covariate -0.6
  • A. Each one-unit increase in exposure is associated with 2.4 more units of outcome, holding covariate constant
  • B. Each one-unit increase in exposure is associated with 2.4 more units of outcome, ignoring covariate
  • C. Covariate must be held at zero for the 2.4 to apply
Answer: A
In multiple regression, each slope is read "holding the other predictors constant" (also called adjusting for or controlling for them).
Question 3Confounding

  • A. The confounder is a mediator and should be removed

Question 4Mediators

You want the total effect of exposure on outcome. Variable M lies on the pathway exposure → M → outcome. Should you adjust for M?

M is caused by exposure and affects outcome.
  • A. Yes, because it improves R-squared
  • B. No, because adjusting for M blocks part of the total effect you want to estimate
  • C. Yes, because M is related to both exposure and outcome
Answer: B
Mediators sit on the causal pathway. Adjusting for them estimates a direct effect, not the total effect running through M.
Question 5Interactions

Two groups are plotted below. Which model structure matches the right panel?

  • A. y ~ x + group
  • B. y ~ x + group + x:group
  • C. y ~ x
Answer: B
An additive model gives parallel lines (same slope, different intercepts). An interaction lets each group have its own slope.
Question 6R syntax

In R, how do you write an interaction between predictors x and group in an lm() formula?

# Which formula includes the interaction term? lm(y ~ x + group + ________, data = dat)
The shorthand x * group expands to main effects plus interaction.
  • A. x - group
  • B. x:group
  • C. x / group
Answer: B
A colon between two variables creates the interaction term. Asterisk (x * group) adds main effects and interaction together.

Question 8Study design

When estimating an exposure effect, modern guidelines recommend choosing adjustment variables:

  • A. After fitting many models and keeping variables that change the estimate most
  • B. Ahead of time, based on subject-matter knowledge (e.g. a DAG)
  • C. Only if they are statistically significant in bivariate tests
Answer: B
For effect estimation, the adjustment set should be chosen a priori. Data-driven selection is more defensible for prediction than explanation.

Question 10Causal claims
