Multiple Linear Regression Prework Quiz

10 MCQs for revising adjusted effects, confounding, R-squared, prediction, and interactions before Workshop 8.

Question 1

Which formula fits a multiple linear regression predicting FEV from smoking, age, and height?

Outcome: fev_l. Predictors: smoke, age_yrs, height_in.

  • A. lm(smoke ~ fev_l + age_yrs + height_in, data = fev)
  • B. lm(fev_l ~ smoke + age_yrs + height_in, data = fev)
  • C. lm(fev_l + smoke ~ age_yrs + height_in, data = fev)
Answer: B. The outcome goes on the left of ~. Predictors go on the right, joined with +.
Question 2

What does the raw smoking coefficient say?

Model: lm(fev_l ~ smoke, data = fev). Reference group: non-smokers.

  • A. Smokers have predicted FEV 0.711 litres higher than non-smokers in the raw model.
  • B. Smoking causes FEV to increase by 0.711 litres.
  • C. Smokers have predicted FEV 2.566 litres higher than non-smokers.
Answer: A. The coefficient is the raw smoker minus non-smoker difference. It is not automatically causal.
Question 3

Which DAG shows confounding by age?

  • A. Age points to smoking and to FEV.
  • B. Smoking points to age and FEV.
  • C. Smoking causes age.
Answer: A. A confounder is related to both the exposure and the outcome, and it should not be caused by the exposure.

Question 6

Which variable would usually be a mediator, not a confounder?

  • A. Age, because age happens before smoking.
  • B. Airway irritation caused by smoking, because it is on the pathway to FEV.
  • C. Sex, because it is a background characteristic.
Answer: B. If estimating the total smoking effect, adjusting for a mediator can remove part of the effect you want to estimate.
Question 7

What does adjusted R-squared help with?

  • B. It is the p-value for the smoking coefficient.

Answer: A. R-squared never decreases when predictors are added. Adjusted R-squared penalizes unnecessary predictors.
Question 8

Which prediction setup compares smokers and non-smokers fairly?

Model: fev_l ~ smoke + age_yrs + height_in + sex

  • A. Change smoking status only, while keeping age, height, and sex the same.
  • B. Compare a 9-year-old non-smoker with a 17-year-old smoker.
  • C. Compare the tallest smoker with the shortest non-smoker.
Answer: A. To isolate the smoking contrast in predictions, hold the other predictors fixed.

Question 10

Which final reporting sentence is most careful?

  • A. Smoking definitely causes lower FEV by exactly 0.087 litres.
  • B. In this observational dataset, smoking was associated with about 0.087 litres lower predicted FEV after adjusting for age, height, and sex.
  • C. The raw model proves smoking improves lung function.
Answer: B. This states the adjusted association, gives the effect size, names the controls, and avoids overclaiming causation.
